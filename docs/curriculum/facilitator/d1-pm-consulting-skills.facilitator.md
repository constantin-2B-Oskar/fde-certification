## Objectives

By 17:30 every participant can:

1. Turn a complaint into a testable problem: quantified gap, MECE hypothesis tree with priors and tests, first test chosen for value.
2. Write an answer-first memo and a ghost deck whose titles alone tell the story.
3. Read what a person is measured on, fears, controls and knows; ask open questions and listen.
4. Recognize differences in how people take in information and decide (MBTI as a vocabulary) and adapt the first sentence, never the facts.
5. Place people on the curve of change and the adoption curve, and name what the system does to each person's work.
6. Design the target system of a change: sponsors, agents, targets, advocates, and the gaps.
7. Keep one source of truth for the engagement in the Open Knowledge Format, with every email, file, discussion and recording captured, with consent.
8. Sequence a difficult message so that nobody hears news about their own job second, and brief an impatient director in two minutes.

## Two things not to reveal today

Both return on Day 4 and lose their power if they leak now.

1. **Corinne's overrides help.** Backtesting shows the schedule she actually runs delivers about 3 OTIF points more than the official ERP schedule. Hervé's culprit is innocent; his theory is inverted. Today, teams should design the test (backtest executed against official), never get the result.
2. **The consolidation rumor.** Corporate is quietly evaluating a two-site consolidation; that is Hervé's real "why now". Today, reward any team that writes "why now?" as a question to ask. Do not answer it.

If a participant asks directly, say: "Good question. That is exactly what you would go and find out on site."

### Exercise 1, model answer

**Rungs.**

| Rung | Model answer |
|---|---|
| Complaint | "The problem is planning; Corinne is overwhelmed; we need an APS." |
| Symptom | The schedule is rewritten daily; the Friday plan is dead by Monday morning; customers call the director directly. |
| Gap | OTIF on promise date 84% in April against a 95% target and 91% in September: minus one point a month, steadily, for seven months. Plan adherence is unknown: measure it (Friday plan against executed, four weeks, from the ERP and Corinne's file). |
| Candidate causes | The tree below. |
| Problem statement | "OTIF fell from 91% to 84% between September and April while retail orders grew from 12% to 31% of volume and changeovers nearly doubled. By Thursday we will know whether the loss comes from the order pattern, an infeasible schedule, or planning execution, each tested with data the plant controller accepts." |
| Kill test | If OTIF loss tracks changeover growth and the ERP's changeover times diverge from the logged ones, branches A and B hold. If the executed schedule performs worse than the official one, branch C rises. |

**Question behind the question.** Who hurts: Hervé (OTIF and EBIT are his two lines, and he has staked his credibility on the APS theory with the COO); Corinne (blame, and possibly her job). Why now: a 95% corporate target and something upstream; a strong team writes "ask Hervé why this quarter" (the hidden answer is the consolidation review). Solved, in Hervé's words: OTIF back above 90% within a quarter, with a story that holds in front of corporate.

**Model tree** (cut by where on-time delivery is lost: what is ordered, what can be produced, how it is planned).

| Branch (prior) | Leaf | Test | Cost, owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. The order pattern outgrew the plant's effective capacity (high) | A1. Retail orders doubled the changeovers | Changeovers a week against OTIF by month | Data in hand, one hour, FDE |
| | A2. Changeovers ate the bottleneck's capacity below the order book | Extrusion hours lost to changeovers against demand, from the shift logs | Two hours, FDE |
| B. The official schedule cannot be executed as written (high) | B1. The ERP's flat 30-minute changeover is fiction | Logged changeover minutes by product pair against the ERP standard | Half a day, FDE and Corinne |
| | B2. Standard rates are wrong on some lines | ERP rate against demonstrated rate, per line | Two hours, FDE |
| C. Planning execution, Hervé's hypothesis (medium, written visibly) | C1. Manual resequencing hurts OTIF | Backtest: OTIF of the executed sequence against the official ERP schedule, four weeks | One to two days, FDE with Corinne |
| | C2. Planning workload exceeds one person | Shadow Corinne for one morning: hours on expedites against hours on planning | Half a day, FDE |
| | C3. Informal priority rules push other orders late | Late orders by client type | Two hours, FDE |

**First test.** The best answer runs A1 on Monday morning (free, data in hand) and launches the C1 backtest the same day, because it tests the sponsor's theory head-on with evidence he will accept, and its answer changes the recommendation either way. Accept B1 first if well argued (it explains why the plan dies by Monday).

**Decision point.** Thursday noon: choose between a sequencing and capacity workstream, a planning-capacity workstream, or both, based on A1, B1 and C1.

**Typical errors to name in the debrief.** "We need an APS" kept as a branch (embedded solution). Branches that overlap ("capacity" and "changeovers" side by side). Leaves with no test. Hervé's culprit either missing (dismissed) or as the whole tree (accepted). No "why now".

### Exercise 2, model answer

**Governing thought.** "On-time delivery is falling because the retail order pattern is eating the capacity of extrusion, our constraint, through changeovers the ERP does not see; two weeks of measurement and a sequencing test on two lines will tell Hervé which fix to fund before he buys anything."

Accept any version that holds the mechanism (changeovers at the constraint), a number, and a decision. Push back on governing thoughts that declare the APS useless or the planner innocent: the fact base does not prove either, and saying so would cost the team its sponsor.

**Model memo (about 200 words).**

> **To:** Hervé **Subject:** OTIF is losing to changeovers at extrusion; two weeks to confirm the fix
>
> Polymex promised its customers 95% on-time delivery and ran at 91% in September.
>
> Since then, OTIF has fallen to 84% while the two retail clients grew to 31% of volume. Their small, frequent orders have pushed changeovers on extrusion, the plant's constraint, from 49 to 71 a week. Changeovers now take 10.3% of extrusion time against 7.5% in January, which cost about 270 tonnes of good output in March alone. Two lines lose more: M07 about 376 tonnes a quarter to stops nobody records, and M04 runs 15 points slower at weekends. The ERP plans every changeover at 30 minutes, so the official schedule cannot be executed as written.
>
> We recommend two weeks of measurement before any tool decision: real changeover times by product pair, a family-aware sequencing test on two lines, and the M07 and M04 losses with their operators. What it is worth depends on whether freed capacity can be sold; sales says two accounts would take 6% more volume if lead times improved, which would be worth up to about €2.3M of contribution a year. We will confirm that number with Mireille's team.
>
> We need one decision this week: your go for the two-week test on two lines.

**Model ghost deck.**

| # | Action title | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OTIF fell seven points in seven months as retail orders grew to almost a third of volume | Facts 1 and context: OTIF series, retail share |
| 2 | Extrusion is the plant's constraint: every other stage waits for it | Fact 2: starved and blocked minutes by stage |
| 3 | Changeovers now take 10% of extrusion time, up from 7.5%, and cost about 270 tonnes in March | Facts 3 to 6 |
| 4 | The ERP plans every changeover at 30 minutes, so the official schedule cannot be executed | Fact 12, plus logged changeover minutes |
| 5 | Two lines lose another 400 tonnes a quarter to stops and drift that no report shows | Facts 7 and 8 |
| 6 | Two weeks of measurement and a sequencing test on two lines will tell you which fix to fund | Recommendation, value range, decision |

**Value logic for the debrief.** Good tonnes recovered are worth contribution margin only if they can be sold. The plant runs at 91% of demonstrated capacity, so it is not sold out; the honest value sits in the volume that shorter lead times would win (6% more volume × €148M × about 26% contribution ≈ €2.3M a year) and in avoided overtime. Teams that multiplied 272 tonnes by €450 and called it a monthly gain made the classic error; tell them it returns tomorrow morning as "the demand fork".

### One source of truth (slides 71 to 82)

The message: everything the team learns on an engagement (every email, file, discussion and meeting recording) goes into one repository per engagement, in the Open Knowledge Format, the same day. It is how a team of four stays consistent in front of a CFO, how a new FDE becomes useful in days, how coding agents get the right context, and what the client keeps at handover.

**Slide 73.** Ask who has ever searched three inboxes for a decision. Every hand goes up.

**Slide 75.** "Everything" is meant literally: every email, every file, every discussion, every meeting recording. The concept note is what makes a recording findable; the original stays linked.

**Slide 76.** Non-negotiable. Recording needs everyone's consent, announced at the start. People notes stay factual: roles, requests, decisions. Tie it back to Part 5: never write a type about someone.

**Slides 77 and 78.** OKF is young: version 0.1 was published by Google Cloud on 12 June 2026 and version 0.2 on 24 July 2026. Say it is a v0 standard: the value is in a shared, open, plain-file format, not in its maturity. Before delivery, check the field names on the slide against the current SPEC.md in the GoogleCloudPlatform/open-knowledge-format repository; only `type` is required, and `index.md` and `log.md` are reserved filenames.

**Slide 79.** The bundle lives in the team's repository from today, under `knowledge/`. From Tuesday, every lab adds to it: the leak register and assumptions (Day 2), the systems map and the rules file (Day 3), the data contract and the role-play notes (Day 4).

**Slide 81.** Link to this morning's setup: `CLAUDE.md` tells the agent to read `knowledge/index.md` first.

**Slide 82, model answer.** Five files with a `type` each: `people/herve.md` (type: person; role, what he asked in his email, linked to the source); `findings/extrusion-is-the-constraint.md` (type: finding; the starved and blocked minutes, linked to `stage_daily.csv` and the Verifier's log); `metrics/oee.md` (already in the repository; check it); `sources/emails/2026-05-04-herve.md` (type: email; the original text); `decisions/...md` (type: decision; who, when, evidence). ### The interests of people (slides 90 to 96)

**Slide 92.** Walk the table with Polymex: re-keying (Sylvie, removed), Friday checking (Corinne, disliked, removed), rules (Corinne, changed), performance by shift (Marc and the crews, visible).

**Slide 93.** The key slide of this part. The point is practical and ethical at once: when the system automates someone's task, their interest diverges from the project's. **Slide 96.** In France, a change to work organization or a tool that can monitor employees requires informing and consulting the CSE beforehand. Never improvise this; ask HR.

### Hervé role card (for the trainer)

Play him for four minutes per team.

- Courteous, short on time, measured on OTIF and EBIT only.
- After 30 seconds of preamble, interrupt: "What do you need from me?"
- If the team leads with the answer and a number on OTIF or EBIT, lean in and ask one hard question: "And the APS?"
- If the team says or implies that his planner theory is wrong, go cold: "So you are telling me I misread my own plant." Watch whether they recover by reframing his theory as a reasonable reading of incomplete data and offering him the test.
- If they offer options with a recommendation and ask for one decision, agree to the two-week test.
- If a team asks a sharp "why now, why this quarter?", say only: "Let's say corporate is watching this site closely." Do not reveal the consolidation.

### Debrief (slide 122)

Ask the two observer teams: "What moved him? What lost him?" Write the answers on the board in two columns, then show the slide. End on the line: "You will meet Hervé again on Thursday, and the data will not agree with him."
