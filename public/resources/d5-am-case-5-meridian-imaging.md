FDE School, by Devoteam and OSS Ventures. ## The company

| | |
|---|---|
| What it does | Office printers and copiers, managed print services (the customer pays per page; Meridian owns the uptime), and IT services for small and mid-sized companies |
| Revenue | About $5.3 billion, falling a few percent a year |
| People | About 17,000, of whom a large share are field technicians |
| Customers | Companies and public bodies in about 150 countries |
| Ownership | Listed in the US; under pressure from investors to generate cash and pay down debt |

## Strategic positioning

- **Where it wins.** A very large fleet of devices under service contracts: about four fifths of revenue comes after the sale (service, supplies, pages). Customers buy uptime.
- **What is changing.** Offices print less every year. Meridian is moving into IT services and has just **acquired a smaller printer maker**, which adds about 40% to the fleet it services in Europe.
- **The pressures.** Revenue falls while cash is needed. Field service is the largest cost line after the sale. The two service organizations must be merged within a year.
- **What the exco wants.** Absorb the acquired fleet with the same service quality, and show investors that the integration pays.

## What was sold in pre-sales

The contract was signed by the VP Service Europe. It covers **12 weeks in the French service region**, with a team of four FDEs (you).

**Scope in the signed proposal:** "Raise the first-time fix rate from 72% to 85% with predictive maintenance on the whole fleet: device telemetry predicts failures, and each technician's van carries the right parts before the call."

**What the CFO Europe said at the closing meeting:** "At 85% first-time fix, we can work with 150 fewer technicians in France. That is the business case."
