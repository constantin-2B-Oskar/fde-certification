## A kickoff answers four questions for the executive committee

1. **Why are we here?** Your strategy, the problem, the value at stake and its condition.
2. **What will happen?** Twelve weeks, the gates, what you will see and when.
3. **How will we know it worked?** A baseline your controller computes, a gain your CFO signs.
4. **What do you need from me?** One named act per person, with a date.

---
<!-- 6 | Detail -->
## Pre-sales promised something. The kickoff is where you correct it, never week 6.

- Read the proposal against the data annex, line by line.
- Look for the traps: a unit, two definitions of the same number, a person who was never in the room, data that does not exist, a calendar that collides.
- Say what you correct, why, and what you propose instead.
- A promise corrected on day one costs a hard meeting. Discovered at week 6, it costs the project.

## Your brief: from the pack to the kickoff in 90 minutes

1. Read the pack alone for ten minutes, without talking.
2. What was sold, and what is true, by 9:55.
3. The kickoff document, eight slides at most, checked by the Verifier, by 10:45.
4. Rehearse while the next team reads your executive committee.
5. Kickoffs from 11:00: eight minutes, then four of questions.

---
<!-- 9 | Detail -->
## The executive committee will ask you five questions

1. What exactly did we buy, and what are you telling us we did not buy?
2. What is your baseline, and who computes it?
3. What would make you stop at week 4?
4. What do you need from me, personally, this week?
5. What do you not know yet, and how will you find out?
