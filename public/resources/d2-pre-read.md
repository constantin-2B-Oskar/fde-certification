## Main terms

**Plant P&L**: The plant's profit and loss statement, down to plant EBIT, the number the director is judged on. Polymex earns €9.7M of EBIT on €148M of revenue.

**Contribution margin**: Revenue minus variable costs. It answers "what is one more unit worth?" At Polymex one more tonne sold is worth about €447, not its price of €1,721 or its full cost of €1,608.

**Bottleneck (constraint)**: The step that limits the output of the whole plant. An hour lost there is lost for the whole plant; an hour saved elsewhere is a mirage. At Polymex it is extrusion.

**Demand fork**: The question that decides what a freed hour is worth: is the plant sold out? If yes, it is worth the contribution of the extra tonnes. If not, only the variable cost it avoids, such as overtime, unless shorter lead times win orders sales can name.

**Leak**: A place where the plant loses money. Plants leak through five families: scrap and quality, changeovers, unplanned downtime, inventory, and expedites and overtime. The last is usually a symptom.

**Double count**: Valuing the same tonne or hour twice, for example sizing overtime separately from the changeovers it pays for.

**Assumptions table**: The list of every number you did not measure, with its base value, range, source and who confirms it. It is the real deliverable.

**Triangulation**: Building the same figure a second, independent way, such as top-down from the P&L and bottom-up from hours and rates. Within 20 to 30%, present it; further apart, find the error first.

**Six-phase method**: The shape of every FDE engagement: embed, find, build, deploy, drive adoption, expand. Each phase ends on an exit criterion the sponsor can see.

**Project charter**: The document that turns your TIP one-pager into a contract. It adds target and date, scope in and out, kill criteria, named roles, cost and governance.

**Kill criteria**: Measurable conditions, one per gate, that stop the project. Written on day one, before anyone is attached to the solution.

**No-surprises rule**: Bad news reaches the sponsor the same day, with two or three options and your recommendation.

**Baseline**: The agreed reference a gain is measured against: twelve rolling months when there is seasonality, never fewer than six, on a frozen, named perimeter. Settle it with the controller in week one.

**Validated gain**: A gain the client's finance team has signed. It exists when the client's controller computes it without you, following a protocol agreed before any software runs.

## The ideas to hold on to

- One more tonne is worth its contribution margin, never its price or its full cost.
- Value a freed hour with the fork: sold out, not sold out, or lead time that wins named orders.
- The assumptions table, a range and a second path are what make a number credible.
- Six phases, each with an exit the sponsor can see, and kill criteria written on day one.
- The gain is announced by the controller's spreadsheet, not yours, from a protocol signed in week one.

## Questions to think about before the session

1. In a site or project you know, what was the constraint, and how did you know it was that step and not another?
2. Think of a gain you have seen claimed. Who computed it, against which baseline, and would finance have signed it?
3. When a project you worked on went badly, when did the sponsor first hear the bad news, and how?
