## Strategic positioning

- **Where it wins.** Own brands at accessible prices. Peakline controls the chain from design to store, so it is cheaper than the big brands and faster than most retailers.
- **What is changing.** Online pure players and specialist brands are taking the top of the market. Margins are under pressure.
- **The pressures.** Commitments to cut CO2 per product and to repair and resell more. Long supply chains from Asia (10 to 14 weeks by sea) make the group slow to react to demand.
- **What the exco decided.** Bring part of the assembly back to Europe, starting with bicycles, to be faster and less exposed. The bike plant is the test case for the whole group.

## What was sold in pre-sales

The contract was signed last month by the head of the cycling business. It covers **12 weeks at the Peakline Cycle Works plant in northern France**, starting next Monday (mid-November), with a team of four FDEs (you).

**Scope in the signed statement of work:** "Predict component shortages on the assembly lines and re-sequence the daily plan around them, so that lines stop less often. Pilot on two lines, then all eight."

**What the pre-sales demo showed:** an AI forecast of bike sales per store, with fewer empty shelves. Several exco members left the demo saying "the project will fix our store stockouts". The demo is not in the signed scope.
