FDE School, by Devoteam and OSS Ventures. ## Strategic positioning

- **Where it wins.** Thousands of formulations, made to order, faster than the commodity giants. Customers stay because Halden matches a color or a hardness in days, not weeks.
- **What is changing.** Customers order smaller lots, more often. Made-to-order (MTO) is now 45% of vinyl volume, up from 30% five years ago. Lead times have slipped to 9 to 14 days; key customers ask for 5.
- **The pressures.** Raw material prices swing month to month. Two experienced planners will retire within two years, and their sequencing rules are in their heads. European plants pay three times the US energy price.
- **What the family wants.** Grow the specialty share, protect service, keep the culture: no layoffs in the company's history, and the family intends to keep it that way.

## What was sold in pre-sales

The contract was signed three weeks ago by the COO. It covers **12 weeks at the lead vinyl plant, Riverside**, with a team of four FDEs (you) and a fixed fee.

**Scope in the signed proposal:** "A sequence-aware scheduling system for Riverside's six compounding lines, built with the planners, running in parallel to the current plan by week 8 and live by week 12. Typical results: 30% fewer changeover hours on made-to-order families."

**What the sales lead wrote to the CEO** after the last meeting:

> "Once Riverside is live, we can roll this out to all 18 plants by the end of the year. Our estimate: $8M a year for the group."

Nobody from group IT attended the pre-sales meetings.

The other two vinyl plants (Ohio and the Netherlands) have different lines, different ERP set-ups, and no one has looked at their changeover data. The 15 other plants make other products.
