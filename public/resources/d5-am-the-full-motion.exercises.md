# Day 5 morning: exercises

FDE School, by Devoteam and OSS Ventures. Participant handout. Five teams, five companies: team 1 takes case 1 (Halden Polymers), team 2 case 2 (Peakline), team 3 case 3 (Groupe Ardent), team 4 case 4 (Laboratoires Sorel), team 5 case 5 (Meridian Imaging). Each case is inspired by a real company and disguised: add nothing that is not in your pack.

## The situation

Pre-sales is over and the contract is signed. On Monday at 9:00 you, the FDE team, open the project in front of the client's executive committee. Your **kickoff document** tells them what will happen over the next 12 weeks, how everyone will know it worked, and what you need from each of them. It is also your one chance to correct, politely and with reasons, what pre-sales promised and the data does not support.

## Timing

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 9:15 | Read the pack alone, ten minutes, no talking |
| 9:25 | **What was sold, and what is true.** List every promise made in pre-sales, check each one against the data annex, and find the traps: units, definitions, missing people, missing data, timing. By 9:55 |
| 9:55 | **The kickoff document**, with the Verifier checking every number. By 10:45 |
| 10:45 | Document frozen. Rehearsal inside the team; meanwhile the next team reads your "Who you present to" table, because it will play your executive committee |
| 11:00 | **Kickoffs**: 8 minutes of presentation, 4 minutes of questions each, teams 1 to 5 in order |
| 12:00 | Lunch |

**The executive committee is played by the trainer and the next team** (team 2 plays case 1's committee, team 3 plays case 2's, and so on; team 1 plays case 5's). Each player takes one person from the table and asks the question that person would ask.

Roles today: the Lead is chosen by the trainer from those who led least this week. The Lead presents, the Verifier answers the number questions, the Storyteller holds the time, the Builder runs Claude and the document.

## The kickoff document (eight slides at most)

| Slide | Title (a full-sentence conclusion) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why we are here | The client's strategic priority in their words, the problem it creates, the value at stake with its condition, and what you ask the committee to decide today |
| 2 | What we will do, and what we will not | The scope as signed. Every pre-sales promise you correct, with the reason and what you propose instead |
| 3 | How we will know it worked | Three to five measures. For each: today's value or "not measured yet", the target, who computes the baseline, and who signs the gain |
| 4 | What happens in the 12 weeks | Phases and dates, what the committee will see and when, the gates and the kill criteria |
| 5 | Who does what | The sponsor and what the sponsor owes, the business owner, the IT owner, the experts' named hours, HR where roles may change, the steering committee's rhythm |
| 6 | What each of you wins | One line per committee member, in their own terms; if someone wins nothing, say so |
| 7 | What we need from you, by when | Access, data, people's time, the IT and security review started in week 1 |
| 8 | Risks, and Monday | The three main risks with their owner, what you will not do, and the first actions of Monday and Tuesday |

## Checklist before 10:45

- Every promise from pre-sales that the data does not support is corrected on slide 2, with the reason. No surprise is left for week 6.
- Every number has a source and a unit, and the units are consistent. The Verifier has recomputed every figure.
- The baseline is computed by the client's controller or finance team, not by you. A one-off cash gain is not presented as a yearly profit gain.
- No gain is framed as fewer people. Roles are decided by HR and managers; say who.
- IT and security are in the plan from week 1, with a named owner.
- Each committee member can find their own win on slide 6.
- The kill criteria are written: what would make you stop at a gate.

## The questions the committee will ask

1. What exactly did we buy, and what are you telling us we did not buy?
2. What is your baseline, and who computes it?
3. What would make you stop at week 4?
4. What do you need from me, personally, this week?
5. What do you not know yet, and how will you find out?
