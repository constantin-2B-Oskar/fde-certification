In the morning, each of the five teams takes one company, inspired by a real one and disguised: a family-owned compounder, a sports brand that makes what it sells, an appliance and cookware group, a pharmaceutical laboratory and a printer company. In each, pre-sales is over and the contract is signed. Your job as the FDE team is to open the project: a kickoff document that tells the client's executive committee what will happen in the next 12 weeks, how everyone will know it worked, and what you need from each of them. As on Days 3 and 4, each team works on one laptop. Another team and the trainer play your executive committee. After lunch, the five kickoffs are debriefed and you write your own 30/60/90 plan, alone, in 15 minutes. The day ends with the FDE Certification exam: 240 questions in 120 minutes, pass mark 65%, closed book, on your own laptop, with no AI tools, no notes and no other tabs. If you do not pass, you can retake it after 24 hours.

## Main terms

**Pre-sales**: Everything that happens before the contract is signed: meetings, a demo, a proposal. It often promises more than the data supports, and the FDE inherits those promises.

**Kickoff**: The meeting that opens a signed project. It tells the client what will happen, how the gain will be measured, who does what, and what is needed from them. Today you present one at 11:00.

**Executive committee**: The client's top team: the CEO and the heads of operations, finance, IT, quality and HR. Each member wants something different from your project.

**Steering committee**: The meeting where the sponsor and key client people decide on gates, scope changes, resources, and whether to go on or stop. Your kickoff sets its rhythm.

**Governing thought**: The single message of your document, on slide 1. It states the client's priority, the value at stake with its condition, and the decision you ask for.

**Changeover**: Switching a machine or line from one product to another, during which it does not produce good parts.

**OEE**: Overall equipment effectiveness: availability × performance × quality.

**Throughput**: The output a team and its machines produce. In every case, every gain is framed as throughput: the same team producing more, never fewer people.

**Baseline**: The agreed reference a gain is measured against. On Friday, the client's controller computes it, not you.

**Run-rate**: The recurring level a gain reaches once fully in place, usually per month or year. It must be distinguished from what has actually been realized so far.

**Validated gain**: A gain that the client's finance team has signed: measurable, attributable and backed by the CFO. Until then it does not count.

**Kill criteria**: Measurable conditions, written on day one, that tell you when to stop at a gate. A kill is a result, written up like a gain.

**Verifier**: The team role that checks every number independently. Today the Verifier recomputes the base case and answers the number questions in front of the committee.

**Operating principles**: The working rules of the week, gathered together on Friday afternoon after the cases.

**30/60/90-day plan**: Your plan for the first three months of a deployment. For each period you name a goal, the artifact you own, the person whose trust you must earn, and how you will know it worked.

## The ideas to hold on to

- Friday asks you to use the whole week in about two hours: the client's strategy, the value and its baseline, the plan with gates, the people and IT.
- Polymex was a rehearsal; these five companies are inspired by real ones, so the confidentiality rules apply: add nothing that is not in the case pack, and if you think you recognize a company, keep it to yourself.
- Pre-sales promised something. The kickoff is where you correct what the data does not support, politely and with reasons, never at week 6.
- Every number needs a source line and a unit, and the baseline belongs to the client's controller.
- When you do not know something in front of the executive committee, say so, and say how you will find out.

## Where this day fits in the week

Days 1 to 4 each gave you one part of the job. Day 1 covered the role and how to structure a problem and a message. Day 2 covered sizing in euros, the six-phase method and the gains validation protocol. Day 3 covered plant systems, data and AI. Day 4 covered what goes wrong on a project, technical and human, and how to scale to other plants. On Day 5 they converge: you use every tool you built this week on real case data, against the clock, and then you plan your own first deployment.

## Questions to think about before the session

1. On a site you know, which number would a controller refuse to sign today, and what baseline would change that?
2. When did you last face a sponsor's request that hid a different problem, and how did you find the real one?
3. What would make you stop a project you are on at week four, and have you written it down?
